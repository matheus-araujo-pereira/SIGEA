\begin{resumo}[Abstract]
\begin{otherlanguage*}{english}
This document establishes the formal modeling and detailed specification of Use Cases for the Intelligent Adverse Event Management System based on the Global Trigger Tool methodology (SIGEA-GTT), developed at the Federal University of Sergipe (UFS). Grounded in the principles of Use-Case Driven Software Engineering and the formal guidelines of the OMG UML 2.5 specification, this work delineates the system boundary and characterizes with precision the four operational actors of the platform: the IT Administrator (\texttt{ADMIN}), the Faculty Advisor and Senior Auditor (\texttt{PROFESSOR}), the Student Clinical Auditor in Training (\texttt{STUDENT}), and the System Temporal Actor (\texttt{SYSTEM\_TIMER}). Both the macro system perspective and four modular diagrams are modeled in native vector TikZ, encompassing security and access governance with institutional domain enforcement (\texttt{@academico.ufs.br}), canonical parametrization of the IHI-GTT catalog (6 modules and 53 triggers) and NCC MERP harm severity scale, academic cohort management, timed 20-minute clinical audit execution using high-fidelity simulated electronic health records structured in semi-structured JSONB, root-cause quality tools (Ishikawa 6M Diagram, GUT Priority Matrix, 5W2H Action Plan, and PDCA Cycle), and formative faculty grading with epidemiological metric consolidation. A total of twenty-two use cases are comprehensively specified following Cockburn's template, covering main, alternative, and exception scenarios, pre- and post-conditions, business rules, and non-functional constraints. Finally, bidirectional traceability matrices are established against all twenty-five functional requirements (RF01 to RF25), ISO/IEC 25010 quality attributes, and physical database entities in PostgreSQL 16 LTS, guaranteeing architectural completeness and implementation fidelity.
