\begin{resumo}[Abstract]
The occurrence of adverse events (AEs) in healthcare facilities represents one of the most critical challenges in global public health, necessitating innovative strategies for education and practice in patient safety. Traditionally, healthcare institutions rely on voluntary reporting systems, which capture less than 10\% of real adverse incidents. In response to this limitation, the \textit{Institute for Healthcare Improvement} (IHI) established the \textit{Global Trigger Tool} (GTT) methodology, structured on the active, retrospective review of medical records through clinical trackers or ``triggers''. This document formalizes the \textbf{Departmental Project} proposal for the development and validation of \textbf{SIGEA-GTT} (\textit{Intelligent System for Adverse Event Management – Global Trigger Tool}), conducted at the Department of Computing (DCOMP) in collaboration with the Department of Nursing at the Federal University of Sergipe (UFS). The project aims to develop an adverse event monitoring system as an educational tool for the safety of hospitalized patients, encompassing: establishing a training environment for nursing undergraduates; generating analytical adverse event reports; providing real-time interactive dashboards; and analyzing the pedagogical efficacy in promoting patient safety. The platform integrates the 6 specialized modules and 53 standardized triggers of the IHI-GTT taxonomy with the NCC MERP harm severity classification matrix (Categories A through I) and established healthcare quality management tools (Ishikawa Diagram, PDCA Cycle, GUT Matrix, SWOT Matrix, 5W2H Method, and Brainstorming). The computational architecture is built on state-of-the-art open-source LTS technologies (Java 21, Spring Boot 3.3, PostgreSQL 16 with native JSONB support, Angular 18, and TailwindCSS), designed for high data-density clinical workstations (WUXGA, HD+, HD, and Ultrawide) and operating on 100\% simulated medical records without direct connectivity to live hospital databases. The 12-month work plan is structured into four sequential phases governed by a strict Quality Gate (100\% functional implementation, 100\% automated test coverage, and 100\% technical documentation), with two formal institutional reports: the 6-Month Partial Report and the 12-Month Final Report, aiming to yield software patent registrations, scientific articles, and book chapters.

\textbf{Keywords}: Health Education. Adverse Events. Risk Factors. Patient Safety. Information Technology.
\end{resumo}
